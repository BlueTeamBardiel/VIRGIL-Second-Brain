# Threat Data and Threat Feeds

## What it is

In **Super Mario Bros.**, the first time you play World 1-1 you walk right into the Goomba and die. The second time you know the Goomba is there. By the tenth playthrough you know the exact frame the Hammer Bro throws, where the hidden 1-Up mushroom lives in 1-2, and which pipe in 4-2 warps you forward. Every run is intelligence collection. Every death is an IoC. The speedrunner who finishes in four minutes flat isn't faster than you — they've ingested a thousand runs of threat data and they know the map. **That's exactly what threat data and threat feeds do — they give defenders the map of the level before the Goomba shows up.**

Plain English: someone else got hit by the bad guy yesterday. They wrote down the bad guy's IP, file hash, domain, and behavior. They published it. You consume that list so when the same bad guy knocks on your door tonight, your SIEM screams and your EDR blocks before the Goomba touches Mario.

Technical: **threat data** is the raw atomic indicators — IPs, domains, URLs, file hashes, CVE IDs, registry keys, user-agent strings. **Threat feeds** are the delivery mechanism — a continuously updated stream of those indicators, usually delivered via [[STIX/TAXII]], JSON over HTTPS, CSV, or a vendor API. **Threat intelligence** is what you get when you enrich threat data with context: who, why, when, how confident, and what to do about it. Data without context is just noise; intelligence is data you can act on.

## Why it matters

CS0-003 Objective 1.4 hits this hard. CompTIA wants you to compare sources, evaluate quality, and explain how feeds drive [[Detection and Monitoring]], [[Vulnerability Management]], and [[Threat Hunting]]. In the real SOC, a feed-less detection program is staring at a black map. You will burn the entire shift triaging your own logs from scratch instead of leveraging the fact that somebody at Mandiant, CISA, or your ISAC already named the threat actor and published the IoCs. Feeds are how the defender community scales — herd immunity for networks.

## Key facts

### Common indicator types in a feed

| Indicator | Example | Lifespan | Pivot value |
|---|---|---|---|
| IP address | `185.220.101.5` | Hours–days | Low (rotates fast) |
| Domain | `update-microsoft[.]co` | Days–weeks | Medium |
| URL | `hxxps://cdn.evil[.]ru/p.ps1` | Days | Medium |
| File hash (SHA-256) | `a3f9...` | Permanent for that exact file | High but fragile (one byte change kills it) |
| CVE ID | `CVE-2026-1234` | Permanent | High for patching |
| TTP / behavior | "encoded PowerShell spawning from Office" | Years | Highest — adversaries hate changing tradecraft |

This stack is the **Pyramid of Pain** in practice. Hashes are easy for the adversary to change, TTPs are painful. A feed that gives you only IPs and hashes is bottom-shelf. A feed that maps to [[MITRE ATT&CK]] techniques is shelf-stable.

### Feed sources — where the data comes from

**Open source (OSINT)** — free, public, variable quality.
- **AlienVault OTX, abuse.ch (URLhaus, MalwareBazaar, ThreatFox), Spamhaus, Emerging Threats Open**
- **Government bulletins** — CISA advisories, US-CERT, NCSC-UK, ACSC. These are gold for nation-state and critical-infrastructure threats. CISA's KEV (Known Exploited Vulnerabilities) catalog is mandatory reading for federal and a sane baseline for everyone else.
- **CERT/CSIRT publications** — national and sector CERTs publish post-incident write-ups with IoCs. Slow but high confidence.
- **Blogs, forums, Mastodon/X infosec community** — fast, sometimes first to publish, low formal confidence until corroborated.
- **Social media** — researchers drop hashes on Twitter/X hours before vendor advisories. Useful early-warning, terrible audit trail.

**Closed source / paid feeds** — Mandiant, Recorded Future, CrowdStrike, Flashpoint, Intel 471.
- Curated, scored, attributed. You pay for the analyst hours, not the raw bytes.
- Lower false positive rate, faster turnaround, deeper context on threat actor groups and campaigns.

**Information sharing organizations (ISACs/ISAOs)** — sector-specific.
- FS-ISAC (financial), H-ISAC (health), E-ISAC (electric). You get peer-shared IoCs from organizations in your sector that just got hit. Membership-gated, NDA-bound, the most relevant feed you can subscribe to.

**Deep/dark web sources** — paste sites, criminal forums, leak sites (LockBit's blog, Cl0p's blog), Telegram channels. Tells you when your data is for sale before your customers tell you. Usually accessed through a paid intel vendor — direct collection is legally and operationally hazardous.

**Internal sources** — your own past incidents, honeypot data, [[SIEM]] correlation hits, EDR detections. The most relevant feed you'll ever have is the one generated by your own network. *The IoC that already hit you once is the IoC most likely to hit you again.*

### Threat actor categories the feeds cover

| Actor | Motivation | Sophistication | What the feed flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nation-state / APT** | Espionage, sabotage, geopolitics | Very high | Long-dwell TTPs, custom malware, supply chain |
| **Organized crime** | Money | High | Ransomware affiliates, BEC, banking trojans |
| **Hacktivists** | Ideology | Mixed | DDoS, defacement, leaks |
| **Insider threat (intentional)** | Revenge, money, ideology | Variable | Behavior anomalies, exfil patterns |
| **Insider (unintentional)** | None — error | N/A | Phishing victims, misconfigurations |
| **Script kiddie** | Clout | Low | Off-the-shelf tools, Metasploit defaults |

### Evaluating a feed — the four-letter test

Before you wire a feed into your [[SOAR]] or auto-block list, ask:

- **Timeliness** — how old is this indicator by the time it lands in your console? An IP burned yesterday is a false-positive factory tomorrow.
- **Relevancy** — does the feed cover your sector, your geography, your tech stack? An OT/SCADA feed is useless to a SaaS company.
- **Accuracy** — what's the false positive rate? Spamhaus is rock solid; some open feeds will blocklist Cloudflare ranges and break your week.
- **Confidence levels** — does each indicator come with a score (high/medium/low, or numeric)? A feed without confidence scoring forces you to treat every entry as gospel, which is how you auto-block your own payroll provider at 9am.

> **CompTIA exam trap:** Timeliness, relevancy, accuracy, and confidence are the four canonical feed-quality criteria. CompTIA will ask which is missing, or which a scenario fails. Memorize all four. Don't confuse "confidence" with "accuracy" — confidence is the publisher's stated certainty about the indicator; accuracy is whether the indicator turns out to be correct in your environment.

### How feeds plug into the SOC

- **Detection and monitoring** — feeds get ingested into the [[SIEM]] as watchlists. A connection to a known C2 IP fires an alert. A hash match on an endpoint fires an EDR detection.
- **Vulnerability management** — CVE feeds prioritize the patch queue. CISA KEV says "this CVE is actively exploited right now" — that jumps the queue past whatever the [[CVSS]] score says.
- **Threat hunting** — TTPs from a feed become hypotheses. "APT29 uses scheduled tasks named `GoogleUpdateTaskMachine` for persistence — let's search every endpoint for that pattern."
- **Active defense** — high-confidence IoCs feed [[Honeypot]] tuning, deception infrastructure, and automated blocklists at the firewall and DNS layer.
- **Incident response** — during a live incident, a feed tells you whether what you're seeing is a known campaign, which gives you the playbook for free.

### Information sharing standards

- **STIX** (Structured Threat Information Expression) — JSON schema describing indicators, threat actors, campaigns, TTPs, relationships.
- **TAXII** (Trusted Automated Exchange of Indicator Information) — the transport protocol that delivers STIX. Think of STIX as the package and TAXII as the truck.
- **MISP** — open-source threat sharing platform widely used in Europe and by CERTs.
- **OpenIOC** — older Mandiant XML format, still around.

> **CompTIA exam trap:** STIX is the language, TAXII is the transport. CompTIA loves to swap them. Mnemonic: **S**TIX = **S**tructure, **T**AXII = **T**ransport.

### Where feeds fail you

- **Business-critical assets** sometimes match indicators by coincidence — your SAP host shares a /24 with a botnet. Always allowlist with knowledge of your own asset inventory.
- **Isolated networks** (air-gapped OT, classified enclaves) can't pull a live feed. You need a manual sneakernet or a one-way diode-fed update process.
- **Supply chain** threats often don't show up in feeds until weeks after compromise — SolarWinds was in the wild for ~9 months before the IoCs were public. *Feeds are a floor, not a ceiling — they catch what's already known, not what's hunting you tonight.*
- **Configurations and misconfigurations** are not in any feed. CIS Benchmarks and CSPM tools cover that lane, not threat intel.

## SOC reality

- The L1 console at 3am shows: `THREAT_FEED_MATCH: outbound DNS to known-bad domain, severity HIGH, source: workstation-DESK-4471, feed: Mandiant`. First action is verify the asset, check if the connection actually completed, pull the EDR process tree on that host. Don't auto-isolate on a single feed hit unless your runbook says so — feeds have false positives.
- The CISO does not ask "which feed?" The CISO asks **"is it actually on our network, did anything leave, who else is affected, and is it contained?"** Have those four answers before you escalate.
- Never promise leadership "we blocked it at the firewall so we're safe." The IP got blocked. The domain rotated an hour ago. The actor is on a new piece of infrastructure. *A feed match is a starting pistol, not a finish line.*
- The handoff: L1 triages the feed match and confirms or dismisses. L2 pivots — same IoC across the fleet, related indicators, timeline. IR team owns it once confirmed as an incident. Intel team retros: did our feeds catch this, or did we miss the window and find it via internal hunt?
- 80% of feed hits are noise (stale IPs, shared hosting, legitimate research traffic). The 20% that matter are the entire reason the feeds exist. Tune aggressively or drown.
