# User Training

## What it is

In Death Stranding, before Sam delivers a fragile cargo through BT territory, the game forces you through tutorials: balance with L2/R2, scan with the Odradek, avoid timefall, never piss in a populated area. Skip the training and you'll faceplant down a cliff with a heart-shaped package full of someone's grandmother. That's exactly what user training does — it teaches employees the survival mechanics of working in a hostile network before they hand-deliver credentials to a phishing site.

**User training** is the formal, recurring instruction of personnel on security policies, threat recognition, and safe operational procedures, classified as a **preventive administrative control** under SY0-701.

## Why it matters

The user is the most exploited attack surface in modern security; phishing, BEC, and social engineering bypass every firewall you bought. CompTIA explicitly lists user training under Objective **5.6 — "Given a scenario, implement security awareness practices,"** which means the exam will hand you a scenario (a clicked link, a leaked password, a tailgater) and demand you pick training as the *administrative* mitigation. The trap CompTIA sets: confusing **security awareness** (broad cultural reminders) with **role-based training** (specific to job duties) — and confusing both with **technical controls**. Training is administrative. Always.

## Key facts

### Training categories (know these cold)

| Type | Audience | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **[[Security awareness training]]** | All employees | Annual phishing module, password hygiene |
| **[[Role-based training]]** | Specific job functions | Developers learn [[secure coding]]; finance learns [[BEC]] red flags |
| **[[Onboarding training]]** | New hires | [[AUP]] sign-off, badge usage, reporting |
| **Recurring training** | All staff, periodic | Annual or quarterly refreshers |

### Core topics CompTIA expects (Objective 5.6)

- **[[Phishing]]** recognition — including [[spear phishing]], [[whaling]], [[smishing]], [[vishing]]
- **[[Anomalous behavior]] recognition** — risky, unexpected, or unintentional actions
- **[[Insider threat]]** awareness
- **[[Password management]]** — complexity, reuse, [[password manager]] adoption
- **[[Removable media]]** and cables — the dropped-USB trick
- **[[Social engineering]]** — pretexting, [[tailgating]], [[piggybacking]], [[shoulder surfing]]
- **[[Operational security]] (OPSEC)** — what not to post, say, or leave on a screen
- **[[Hybrid work]] / [[remote work]] environments** — home network hygiene, [[VPN]] use
- **Reporting procedures** — who to call when something feels wrong

### Measuring effectiveness

- **[[Phishing simulations]]** — controlled fake phish; track click-through rate
- **[[Reporting metrics]]** — time-to-report, percentage of users reporting
- **[[Computer-based training (CBT)]] completion rates**
- **Development and execution** — training must be *built*, *delivered*, and *measured*; CompTIA wants all three phases

### The [[Acceptable Use Policy]] (AUP) connection

Training is how you make the AUP real. A signed AUP without training is a legal artifact, not a control.

### Exam traps

- **"Best preventive control against phishing?"** → User training (admin) beats email filters (technical) in scenarios where the question stresses *human judgment*. Read carefully.
- **"User clicked the link anyway"** → The fix is *more frequent* training and **phishing campaigns**, not firing the user.
- **Awareness ≠ Training.** Awareness is posters and emails. Training is structured instruction with assessment.
