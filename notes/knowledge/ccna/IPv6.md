# IPv6

## What it is

IPv4 is the original 32-character gamertag system from 1999 — every good name got claimed years ago and people are paying real money to squat on the leftovers. IPv6 is the platform finally saying "fine, here's a 128-character namespace, go nuts." There are enough IPv6 addresses (~3.4×10³⁸) that you could assign one to every grain of sand on Earth and still have a comical surplus left over. IPv4 caps out at roughly 4.3 billion, which sounded infinite in the 80s and ran out around the time TikTok was invented.

Technically, IPv6 is a 128-bit addressing scheme written in colon-separated hexadecimal — eight groups of four hex digits, like `2001:0db8:85a3:0000:0000:8a2e:0370:7334`. Compare that to IPv4's familiar `192.168.1.10` dotted decimal and you can feel the upgrade.

## Why it matters

Address exhaustion is the obvious driver, but IPv6 also rewires assumptions baked into every network tool built in the last 30 years. ARP is gone. NAT becomes optional. IPsec was supposed to be mandatory. Your firewall rules, your IDS signatures, your inventory scripts — all of them quietly assume IPv4 unless someone went out of their way to teach them otherwise.

That assumption is exploitable. Run IPv4 and IPv6 side-by-side (dual-stack) on a network where the security team only watches IPv4, and you've handed an attacker a second door that nobody guards. It's the Tarkov equivalent of fortifying the main extract while leaving a side exfil completely unmonitored — the loot still leaves the map, you just don't see it happen.

## Key facts

### Addressing

- **128-bit addresses, hex notation**: eight 16-bit groups separated by colons. The space is so large (3.4×10³⁸) that scanning a single /64 subnet the way you'd nmap a /24 is effectively impossible.
- **IPv4 vs IPv6 scale**: 4.3 billion vs 3.4×10³⁸. Like comparing a single Minecraft world to every Minecraft world ever generated by every player simultaneously, then cubed.
- **Loopback is `::1`** in IPv6, the equivalent of `127.0.0.1` in IPv4. Same "talk to yourself" purpose, much shorter to type.

### Link-local addresses

- **`fe80::/10` prefix**: every IPv6 interface auto-assigns itself a link-local address the moment it comes up. No DHCP, no config, no asking permission — like how your Switch instantly sees other Switches in local play without joining a network.
- **Not routed**: link-local stays on the local segment. Routers will not forward these packets, period. Useful for neighbor discovery, useless for reaching the internet.

### NDP (Neighbor Discovery Protocol)

- **Replaces ARP**: in IPv4, ARP maps IPs to MAC addresses. In IPv6, NDP does that job plus router discovery, address autoconfiguration, and redirects.
- **Spoofable**: NDP inherits ARP's original sin — it trusts whoever answers first. NDP spoofing is the IPv6 cousin of ARP poisoning, and it works for the same reason: no authentication on the replies. Any rando on the segment can claim to be the gateway, exactly like an Among Us imposter calmly insisting they're the real one.

### IPsec

- **Built into the IPv6 spec natively** — not bolted on. IPv4 had to retrofit IPsec years after the fact, the way old games get raytracing patched in and run worse for it.
- **Originally mandatory**, now effectively optional in practice. The spec was relaxed because forcing every IPv6 stack to implement IPsec turned out to be unrealistic. So in the wild, you can't assume IPv6 traffic is encrypted just because it's IPv6.

### Transition mechanisms

- **Dual-stack**: host runs IPv4 and IPv6 at the same time, picks per-destination. Most common deployment model. Doubles your attack surface if your monitoring is single-stack.
- **6to4**: encapsulates IPv6 packets inside IPv4 to cross IPv4-only networks. Tunneling, not translation.
- **Teredo**: similar idea, but designed to punch through NAT using UDP. Microsoft's contribution to "let IPv6 work behind your home router."
- **Tunneling abuse**: IPv6 can be tunneled inside IPv6 (or inside IPv4) to slip past intrusion detection systems that only inspect the outer header. The IDS sees a boring packet; the payload is something else entirely. Speedrun strat for evading detection if the defender's tooling is IPv4-myopic.
